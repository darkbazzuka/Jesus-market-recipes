What You Will Need

    Power Source: A USB cable with a male connector (type A or C). You can cut this off any old USB cable.

    Heating Element: A "resistance wire" made of nichrome or kanthal. You can find this in pre-made coils or buy the spool yourself. You will need approximately 2-5 centimeters of wire.

    Container: A small, heat-resistant container to hold your material and the wire. You can use an old 510-threaded vape battery atomizer, a plastic bottle, or a small glass vial.

    Insulation: Something to prevent the wire from touching the metal casing. Cotton or fiberglass wicking material works well.

    Mechanical Assembly: A small piece of copper wire or a screw to hold the heating element in place and connect it to the USB power.


Step 1: Prepare the USB Cable


    Cut the USB cable about 2-3 inches from the connector.

    Carefully strip the outer plastic casing to expose the inner wires. You'll typically find three wires: red (+), black (-), and sometimes a white or green data wire.

    You only need the red (+) and black (-) wires. Cut off the data wire. The data wire is not needed for power.

    Strip the insulation off the ends of the red and black wires to expose the bare metal.

    Connect the red wire to the positive terminal of your heating element and the black wire to the negative terminal.


Step 2: Prepare the Heating Element


    Take your resistance wire. For a decent vape, you want it to have a resistance between 1 and 3 ohms.

    To achieve this, you can wrap the wire around a small pencil or a needle to create a coil. The more wraps you make, the higher the resistance will be.

    Once you have your coil, strip the ends of the wire to expose the bare metal.


**Step 3: Assemble the Vape


    Place your heating element inside your chosen container. The coil should be centered and not touching the walls of the container.

    If using a metal container, you'll need to ensure the heating element does not short circuit against the container's walls. Use your insulation material (cotton) to create a barrier between the wire and the container.

    Connect your heating element to the USB cable. Connect the red wire from the USB to one end of the coil, and the black wire to the other end.

    Secure the connections. You can use a small piece of tape or a soldering iron to make a good connection. Soldering is recommended for a more durable and reliable connection.
