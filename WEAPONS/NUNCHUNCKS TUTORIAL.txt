Materials:

Steel rods: Two pieces of square or round steel stock (e.g., 1/2" x 12 inches each). Steel is preferred over aluminum for durability and proper weight distribution.

Chain or cord: A short length of heavy-duty chain (e.g., 4-6 links) or high-tensile nylon cord. For a purely metal look, use two swivel joints connected by a short chain segment.

Swivels: Two metal swivels to prevent the chain from twisting during spins.

Bolt/nut washers: To secure the swivels to the rods.


Tools:

Angle grinder with cutting and grinding discs

Drill with metal drill bits

Wrench set

File or sandpaper

Measuring tape

Safety glasses and gloves

Step-by-Step Instructions

TUTORIAL

1. Cut the Rods

Cut two steel rods to your desired length. Standard nunchucks are typically between 10 to 14 inches per stick. For beginners, shorter sticks (10-12 inches) offer better control. Ensure both rods are exactly the same length for balanced spinning.

2. Smooth the Edges

Use the angle grinder to smooth all cut edges. Then, file down any sharp burrs. Round off the corners of square rods slightly to reduce the chance of catching on clothing or causing accidental deep cuts. The ends should be blunt but not jagged.

3. Drill Holes

Mark one end of each rod (about 1 inch from the tip). Drill a hole through the center of the rod wide enough to fit your bolt securely. Deburr the hole so the bolt head sits flush or slightly recessed.

4. Assemble the Swivels

Attach a swivel to each rod using a bolt and nut. Tighten securely. The swivel allows the chain to rotate freely, preventing tangles during complex maneuvers. If using a chain directly without swivels, ensure it’s lightweight and flexible.

5. Connect the Rods

Connect the two swivels with the chain. Alternatively, use a single piece of chain with loops at each end attached to the rods via bolts. Ensure the connection is tight but allows free rotation. The total length of the chain should allow the sticks to swing freely without hitting each other unnecessarily.

6. Balance Check

Hold one stick. It should feel balanced in your hand. If one stick is significantly heavier, adjust by filing down material or adding small weights to the lighter end. Balanced nunchucks spin more predictably.

7. Finish (Optional)

Apply a coat of paint, powder coating, or rust inhibitor to prevent corrosion. Ensure the finish doesn’t make the surface too slippery or too grippy depending on your preference.

Your done :D
