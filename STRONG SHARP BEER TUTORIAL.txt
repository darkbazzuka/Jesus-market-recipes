What You Need (Ingredients)
Water: ~4.5 liters (regular tap water or bottled spring water).

Dark or Amber Dry Malt Extract (DME): 900g (almost double the normal amount—using Dark or Amber extract gives it a rich, roasted caramel/coffee taste instead of a light watery taste).

High-Alpha Hops: 45g of hop pellets (3x the normal amount; look for varieties like Cascade, Centennial, Magnum, or Chinook for a punchy bitter/citrus/pine bite).

High-Tolerance Brewer’s Yeast: 1 packet of dry ale yeast (like Safale US-05 or Nottingham—these can handle stronger sugar levels without dying early).

For Fizz Later: ~25g of regular white table sugar.

How to Make It (Easy Step-by-Step)
1. Sanitize Everything
Wash your fermenting jug, airlock, funnel, and thermometer, then soak or spray them with no-rinse brewing sanitizer. Any stray bacteria will spoil the batch.

2. Boil the Heavy Malt and Hops (1 Hour)
Bring 4 liters of water to a boil in a large pot (at least 7–8 liters so it doesn't boil over).

Turn off the heat and stir in all 900g of Dark/Amber Dry Malt Extract. Stir until zero clumps remain so it doesn't burn on the bottom.

Turn the heat back on to a gentle boil and start a 60-minute timer. (Watch closely for the first 5 minutes—thick wort foams up fast!)

At 60 minutes left: Drop in 20g of hops (this creates a strong, punchy bitterness).

At 15 minutes left: Drop in 15g of hops (for deep hop flavor).

At 1 minute left: Turn off the stove and drop in the last 10g of hops (this locks in an intense, fresh hop smell).

3. Cool It Down Fast
Put the whole pot into a sink filled with ice and cold water. Stir gently until the liquid drops down to 20°C (68°F). Hot liquid kills yeast instantly.

4. Ferment It (3 Weeks)
Because there is so much sugar in this batch, the yeast needs an extra week to finish eating it all.

Pour the cooled liquid through a sanitized funnel into your fermenting jug. Top it up with cold water if needed to reach the 4-liter line.

Dump the packet of yeast straight in, plug the top, and shake the jug hard for 45 seconds (yeast needs lots of oxygen to tackle a heavy batch).

Put the airlock on top (filled halfway with water or sanitizer) and store the jug in a dark spot at room temperature (18°C–21°C).

Leave it alone for 21 days. It will bubble violently for the first few days—that means it's working.

5. Bottle and Carbonate (2 More Weeks)
Boil 25g of white sugar in a tiny splash of water, let it cool, and pour it into a clean pot.

Slowly pour or siphon your beer from the jug into that pot—leave the thick yeast mud at the bottom behind.

Fill your sanitized beer bottles, leaving about 3 cm of air at the top, and cap them tightly.

Let the bottles sit at room temperature for 14 days so the trapped gas makes the beer fizzy.
