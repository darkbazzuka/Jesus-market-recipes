Honest note up front: this is an edible novelty, not a health food. Tobacco contains nicotine (roughly 2–3% by weight in cured leaf), so treat each piece like a small dose. Start with tiny bites. If you don't handle nicotine well, use decaffeinated-style low-nicotine leaf (some growers sell "mild" varieties) or reduce the ratio below.

👃 WHERE THE TOBACCO COMES FROM (important)
Best source: a local pipe shop or smoke shop. Ask for loose, food-clean leaf — Virginia bright leaf is the mildest and most "chocolate-friendly." Avoid strongly Latakia'd blends (too tar-like for candy).
Check it's additive-free. Cigarette tobacco has sugars, humectants, and flavorings meant to be smoked, not eaten. Pipe/roll-your-own leaf is far cleaner.
Wash and dry the leaf gently (rinse briefly, pat dry, air-dry on a rack for a few hours). You want clean leaf, not dusty barn leaf.
🔥 STEP-BY-STEP
Step 1 — Prep the tobacco (the secret step)
Take your 8–10 g of leaf and break it into small pieces with your hands.
If using the alcohol option: put the pieces in a jar, cover with 1 tsp bourbon/rum, and let steep 2–24 hours (longer = stronger). This extracts the aromatic oils without the harshness.
Pat the leaf as dry as you can. Moisture is the enemy of chocolate — even a little water makes it seize.
Lightly crush the leaf in a mortar until it's a coarse crumb, not powder. You want visible flecks in the final bar — that texture is part of the experience.
Pro move: Toast the crushed leaf in a dry pan for 60 seconds, off-heat, stirring constantly. It wakes up the nutty, roasted notes. Let it cool completely before adding to chocolate.

Step 2 — Make the base ganache
Heat the 30 ml cream + 10 g butter in a small saucepan until just steaming (not boiling).
Pour over the 250 g chopped dark chocolate in a bowl. Wait 60 seconds.
Stir from the center outward until fully smooth and glossy. Add vanilla, salt, espresso if using.
Target working temperature: ~35°C (95°F). If hotter, let it sit; if colder, warm the bowl in a microwave 5-second bursts.
Step 3 — Fold in the tobacco
Add the crushed (and cooled) tobacco to the ganache in three additions.
Fold gently — don't stir violently. You want an even distribution of flecks without pulverizing them.
Taste-test a pea-sized bit now. Adjust:
Too mild → add another gram of leaf
Too harsh → more chocolate next batch, or a drop more cream this one
Step 4 — Mold it
For bars:

Line a small silicone loaf or bar mold with parchment.
Pour the ganache in, tap the mold firmly on the counter to release air bubbles.
Top with a few extra leaf flecks for looks (optional garnish: flaky salt).
For truffles:

Spoon tablespoon-sized mounds onto parchment, flatten slightly.
Or fill mini silicone truffle molds.
Step 5 — Freeze (your specified method)
Pop the tray into the freezer for 2–4 hours until solid.
Freezing (vs. just refrigerating) gives you a shatter-crisp exterior with a dense, fudge-like interior — the contrast is what makes it interesting.
Pop out of molds. They'll be rock hard at first; let them sit at room temperature 10–15 minutes before eating so they soften to a proper candy bite instead of numbing your teeth.

⚠️ SAFETY & PRACTICAL NOTES
Nicotine content estimate: 10 g of cured leaf ≈ 200–300 mg total nicotine split across ~12 bars = roughly 15–25 mg per bar. That's meaningful. Eat half a bar to start.
Kids and pets: keep labeled and away. Nicotine + chocolate (theobromine) is doubly no-go for dogs.
Storage: airtight container, fridge. Lasts 2–3 weeks. The tobacco keeps developing, so day-3 versions often taste better than day-1.
If it seizes (turns grainy when you add tobacco): a little moisture got in. Rescue with 1 tsp of hot cream, re-melt gently, strain out lumps.
Allergy check: if anyone in the house is sensitive to tobacco alkaloids, label clearly.
🎁 SERVING IDEAS
Cut bars into cigar-shaped sticks and serve on a small board with coffee — it reads as "gourmet" instantly.
Truffle version dipped halfway in plain white chocolate for contrast.
Crumble over ice cream (vanilla only — don't fight the flavor).
Pair with a cold stout or a whiskey neat. Genuinely good combo.
